A Row Is a Snapshot

The Sheet Records One Moment

A spreadsheet row is what was saved at the time it was recorded. It is a snapshot, not a live page.

✓What a snapshot is

  • The label, image reference, and destination link saved at record time.
  • A point-in-time record of what the row captured.
  • A starting point to recheck against the current page.

✕What a snapshot is not

  • Not the current state of the page.
  • Not a guarantee that every field is still valid.
  • Not a live monitor or a verified listing.
Frame noteA newer field does not prove the others are still valid. Recheck each field against the current page.

Version Frames

Four Frames for One Check

Separate what was saved, what is current, what actually changed, and what is still open.

Snapshot

What was saved

The label, image reference, and destination link recorded in the sheet at that time.

Current

What the page shows

Title, destination, image, options, price context, and availability as they are now.

Difference

What actually changed

Only the visible changes you observed, without guessing why they happened.

Question

What is still open

Points that still need confirmation, kept as questions rather than permanent labels.

Recheck by Trigger, Not by Promise

Three Moments to Reopen the Page

Recheck on a concrete trigger, not on the promise that the sheet is still accurate.

  • 1

    Before saving a candidate. Reopen the page and confirm the fields before you commit the row.

  • 2

    Before an off-site action. Recheck just before you prepare to leave the site and act on it.

  • 3

    After waiting. Reopen the page again after time has passed and recheck what changed.

✓Confirmation boundary: rechecking on a trigger is how you keep a snapshot from being read as current.

Archive the Question, Not the Claim

Know When to Stop the Frame

  • 1

    Snapshot conflicts with current. The sheet says one thing, the page says another. Stop and record the difference.

  • 2

    Change is unexplained. You can see a change but not its cause. Stop and record what you saw, not why.

  • 3

    One field is newer. A fresh title does not prove the options are still valid. Stop and recheck each field.

  • 4

    A question stays open. A detail remains unconfirmed. Stop and keep it as a question, not a verified label.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an OOPBuy spreadsheet?

An OOPBuy spreadsheet is a shared document that compiles listing references, category groupings, and product links formatted for use with agent shopping services. It is a reference to research, not an official, live, or complete product catalog.

Is this website the official OOPBuy service?

No. This website is an independent informational and navigation resource. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by OOPBuy or Google.

What does Snapshot vs Current mean?

Snapshot is what the spreadsheet saved at the time it was recorded — the label, image reference, and destination link. Current is what you see when you reopen the page now. The method rechecks each field against the current page instead of trusting the snapshot.

Does this site monitor listings or verify them?

No. This resource is not a store, a live monitor, a link-health service, a QC certification tool, a fee tool, or an order dashboard. It does not provide real-time status, online indicators, or verification results.

Which fields should I recheck separately?

Six fields can change separately: title, destination URL, image, options, price context, and availability. A newer field does not prove the others are still valid, so recheck each one against the current page.

When should I recheck a listing?

Recheck before saving a candidate, before preparing an off-site action, and after waiting before you reopen a page. Recheck by trigger, not by promise, so a stale snapshot is not mistaken for a current fact.
